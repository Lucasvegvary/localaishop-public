Why quotes go quiet, and how to tell if your estimates need a follow-up system

Lucas Vegvary · The Local AI Shop · Napa, California · October 10, 2026

You send a quote for a water heater in Fairfield. The customer says thanks, they'll look it over. Then nothing. A week later you can't tell whether they hired someone else, put the job off, or never opened your email. You didn't start this business to chase quotes at ten o'clock at night.

Quotes go quiet for ordinary reasons, and price is only one of them. A quote follow-up system is a way to make sure every quote gets a second look from you, on a schedule, so a sent quote doesn't just sit. Here's how to tell whether you need one.

Why do quotes go quiet after I send them?

Usually because the customer's life kept moving and nobody reached back out. The reasons are ordinary. They're comparing bids. They're waiting on a spouse, a landlord or an insurance company. Your email landed under everything else in the inbox. The job isn't urgent yet.

Sometimes the price really is a no. That's one reason on the list, not the whole list. The rest are mostly timing and attention, and a follow-up can work with both.

Is a quiet quote a lost job?

Not necessarily. A quote with no answer is an open question, not a no.

The only way to find out which one you're holding is to ask, once or twice, politely, and let the customer answer. Until somebody asks, it stays a maybe. And a customer who has to bring it up again may not bother.

How can I tell if my estimates need a follow-up system?

Look back at the quotes you sent over the last few weeks and see what happened after each one. Ask three questions. Which ones did I follow up on, and when? Which ended in a clear yes or no? Which are still sitting there, and do I know why?

You may need a system if any of these sound familiar:

  • You only follow up when you happen to remember.
  • You find old quotes weeks later and wonder what happened.
  • A customer says "I was going to call you" after you'd given up on them.
  • You can't say how many quotes are open right now.

If you already chase every quote, you may need a reminder more than a system. A list and a calendar alert count.

What does a quote follow-up system actually do?

It prompts a check-in on a schedule after a quote goes out, and it stops when the customer answers. There are three versions worth knowing:

  1. A reminder to you only. You see "quote sent Tuesday, no answer" and make the call yourself.
  2. A message to the customer, in wording you approved. A short note that uses their name, mentions the job, and asks if they have questions.
  3. Both. A reminder to you first, then a check-in to the customer if the quote is still sitting.

It can live in a spreadsheet, a calendar, or field-service software you may already pay for. My examples page shows quote follow-up for heating and air, and for glass, windows and doors.

What should I check before I set one up or buy one?

Check who writes the words, what makes it stop, who gets the reply, whether you can pause it, whether it stays in its lane, and, if it texts, whether the phone carriers' approval is in place. Get a plain-words answer to each. This is the list I'd use:

  • Who writes the words? Nothing should reach a customer that you haven't read and approved.
  • What makes it stop? It should stop when the customer replies, books or says no. Ask the seller to show you what ends it.
  • Who gets the reply? And how fast will you see it?
  • Can you pause it for one customer? Some customers are friends, or already talking with you.
  • Does it stay in its lane? A follow-up checks in. Price, dates and scope come back to you.
  • If it goes out by text, is the phone carriers' approval to send business texts in place, and can a customer reply STOP to end it?

Pick the smallest version that gets the second contact to happen. A person still decides which quotes are worth chasing and what to say when the customer writes back.

Common questions

How soon should I follow up on a quote?

Soon enough that the customer still remembers you. I'd start with a few days after the quote goes out, then adjust to the job. A leak repair and a kitchen remodel don't move at the same speed.

Do I need software to follow up on quotes?

No. A list of open quotes and a calendar reminder is a follow-up system. Software helps when the list gets long, but the habit is the part that matters.

Will following up make me look pushy?

Not if it's short, polite and stops once they answer. Write it the way you'd say it on the phone.

The short version

  • Quotes go quiet for ordinary reasons. Price is only one.
  • A quiet quote is an open question, not a no.
  • You may need a follow-up system if you only follow up when you remember, or you can't say how many quotes are open.
  • A list and a calendar reminder count. Software is optional.
  • Before you buy, check who writes the words, what makes it stop, who gets the reply, and whether you can pause it.
