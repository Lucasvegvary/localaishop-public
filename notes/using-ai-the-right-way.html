Using AI the right way when it's your name on the truck

Lucas Vegvary · The Local AI Shop · Napa, California · October 10, 2026

Your name is on the truck. Whatever your AI says to a customer, it says under that name.

So using AI the right way comes down to the same rules you already run your shop by: tell people the truth, protect what they trust you with, and stand behind the work. Here's what that looks like with the new tools.

1. If a customer is talking to a machine, they should know it.

A lot of the tools sold to trade shops right now answer your phone, reply to your texts, or chat on your website. Some of them are good. Some of them are set up to sound like a person named Ashley who doesn't exist.

Skip that. When a bot answers, have it say so in the first line: "Hi, this is the automated after-hours assistant for Carneros Plumbing & Heating. I'll take your details, and someone from the office will call you in the morning." People don't mind a machine that's honest about what it is. They mind finding out later.

For the chat on your website, it's also the law here. California's bot disclosure law makes it illegal to use a bot online to mislead someone about whether they're talking to a person when the point is to sell them something. For phone and text bots, saying so up front is simply the honest move. Either way, it costs you nothing.

2. Customer information stays where it belongs.

Here's the one that catches good people. You're tired, you've got a messy estimate, and you paste the customer's name, address, gate code and phone number into a free chatbot to clean it up.

Before you do that, know where it goes. Many free and personal AI accounts can use your chats to improve their models unless you turn that setting off. Business accounts usually work differently. Either way, check the setting yourself. Don't assume.

My rule for owners: if you wouldn't read it out loud in a crowded coffee shop, don't paste it into a tool you haven't checked. Strip the name and address first. "Customer in Napa, two-story house, water heater in the garage" gets you the same help.

3. The AI doesn't get to make promises you can't keep.

In 2024, Air Canada lost a small-claims case before a British Columbia tribunal because the chatbot on its website told a grieving customer he could claim a bereavement discount after he booked. That wasn't the airline's policy. The airline argued the chatbot was responsible for its own words. The tribunal didn't buy it and ordered the airline to pay.

That's the whole lesson in one case: whatever your AI tells a customer, you told the customer. If your text-back bot quotes a price, that's your price. If it says "someone will be out today," that's your promise.

So set your tools up to collect and confirm, not to quote and commit. Prices, dates, warranties and anything about money go through a person.

4. You own the accounts, and you know where the off switch is.

An honest setup is one you can understand and stop. You should own every login. You should be able to say what each automation does in one sentence. And you should be able to turn any of it off in under a minute without calling anybody.

If someone builds you something and you can't explain it, or you can't turn it off, that's not your system. That's theirs, running inside your business.

5. Use it to give people more of you, not less.

The best use of AI in a small shop isn't replacing the person your customers like dealing with. It's taking the paperwork off that person so there's time left to be good at the job.

The callback happens faster because a tool logged the missed call and texted you the details. The quote goes out tonight instead of Sunday because AI drafted it from your notes and you checked the numbers. The customer still gets you. They just get you sooner.

The short version

  • Tell people when they're talking to a machine.
  • Keep customer details out of tools you haven't checked.
  • Don't let the AI promise anything you wouldn't promise yourself.
  • Own the accounts. Know the off switch.
  • Use it to free up the human, not to hide behind.

None of this is new. It's how a good shop already runs. AI just makes it easier to forget, because the tool sounds so sure of itself.
